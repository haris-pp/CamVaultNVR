If you merge app.py by hand, these are the only edits:
 1. next to "import settings_api" add:
        import playback_api
 2. right after  settings_api.init(...)  add:
        playback_api.init(
            app, get_setting, query_db,
            user_folder=lambda sid: get_user_session_folder(sid),
            needs_transcoding=lambda path: needs_transcoding(path),
        )
 3. in api_recordings(), the time-range filter now compares seconds of the day. Before, a range that
    ended at midnight (to=86400) matched nothing. (Download page benefit; Playback does not use this route.)

How it works
 - Green days come from the day folders that contain .ts files.
 - The slider's green bars are the recorded sessions of that camera and day.
 - Click or drag on the bar: it loads a playlist that starts 2 minutes before that time (so a small step
   back needs no reload) and runs for "Longest range" from Settings. Clicking again inside that range only seeks.
 - A click in a gap jumps to the next footage. Playback carries on over gaps, into the next session,
   and into the next day with footage.
 - Segment lengths come from the file names and last-modified times, so no ffprobe: a 24-hour day lists in
   ~50 ms. Where ffmpeg restarted, the playlist gets #EXT-X-DISCONTINUITY so the player does not stumble.
   This assumes recorder and app run on the same machine (same clock and time zone), as they do now.
 - Settings used: skip seconds, default speed, longest range, 12 / 24-hour clock.

No database change, no new packages, nothing is written except the temporary playlist in user_data/.
Keys: Space / K play-pause, Left / Right skip, F full screen, M mute.
